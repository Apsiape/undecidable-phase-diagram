\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Can the phase diagram of one fixed classical lattice model be uncomputable? For quantum spin systems at zero temperature it can: there are families of Hamiltonians whose spectral gap or ground-state phase cannot be computed from the parameter. We prove a classical counterpart at positive temperature. There is one translation-invariant nearest-neighbour interaction on $\Z^2$, with local energies $0$, $1$ and $+\infty$ (hard constraints) and a fixed universal Turing machine $U$ built in, and there are disjoint intervals $J_n$ with rational endpoints computable from $n$, such that at every inverse temperature $\beta$ in $J_n$ the model has exactly one translation-invariant Gibbs measure if $U$ does not halt on input $n$, and at least two if $U$ halts. So uniqueness of the translation-invariant Gibbs measure at rational inverse temperature is undecidable for this single model. Uniqueness among all Gibbs measures fails at every temperature, because the hard constraints freeze a non-periodic background. These results are proved in Lean, with Dobrushin's uniqueness theorem and the existence of the plus state of the two-dimensional Ising model taken as hypotheses.

The background is a hierarchical tiling that contains arbitrarily large square grids. On each grid a Turing machine reads a label $n$ from the size of the grid and runs $U$ on $n$. Where $U$ halts, small sensors made of free bits and unit energies couple the corner spins of all grids of that size into an ordinary Ising model, whose coupling is large only when $\beta$ is close to $n\log2$. Assuming the exact solution of the two-dimensional Ising model, the inverse temperatures with at least two translation-invariant Gibbs measures form exactly a union of explicit intervals around $n\log 2$ over the halting inputs $n$; restricted to rational $\beta$, this set is $\Sigma^0_1$-complete.
\end{abstract}
